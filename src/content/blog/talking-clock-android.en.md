A talking clock says the time out loud, so you know it without looking at a screen. It helps when your hands are busy, for example while cooking, and for people with low vision. It also helps if you lose track of time once the phone is face down. Android has a couple of ways to do it.

## Ask the assistant

The quickest option is to ask Google's assistant "what time is it?" and it answers out loud. It works well while you remember to ask. When you're deep in something, though, you won't think of asking, and those are the moments when the time slips away.

## Have the screen read to you

TalkBack, Android's screen reader, reads out what's on the screen, the clock included. It's made for blind and low-vision users and it changes how every tap and swipe works, so most people won't want it on all day just to hear the time.

## A clock that speaks on its own

What many people picture when they search for a talking clock is the old kind that announced the hour without being asked. On a phone that takes an app. A good one plays a short sound first so the voice doesn't startle you, and speaks only during the hours you choose. It also has to keep on time when battery saving is on. If your reminders arrive late, read [why Android alarms don't go off on time](/blog/android-alarm-not-going-off-on-time/).

## When hearing the time helps

Hearing the time takes less effort than checking it. Looking at the phone to see the time often turns into opening a message or an app, and the check you meant to take two seconds takes ten minutes. A voice saying "It's 3:30" gives you the time and nothing else. There's more on this in [why hearing the time helps you keep track of it](/blog/hourly-chime-app/).

## Tolli's "Say the time"

[Tolli](/tolli/) is a little bell for Android that dings every 10, 15, 30 or 60 minutes. With "Say the time" turned on, it says the time after each ding, for example "It's 10:45." It uses the voice installed on your phone, in your phone's language, and plays it at alarm volume so you hear it even with the ringer down. It stays quiet outside the hours you set. It's free, with no account and no ads.
