You start sorting the photos on your phone after dinner. You look up and it's 1 a.m., you're thirsty, and you don't remember deciding to keep going.

Researchers call this **hyperfocus**. You concentrate so hard that you stop registering everything else, the clock included.

## What hyperfocus is

In a review published in *Psychological Research*, Brandon Ashinoff and Ahmad Abu-Akel point out that many studies use the word without saying what they mean by it. They also compare it with *flow*, the state where a task absorbs you.

Most research on hyperfocus looks at ADHD and autism. At the University of Michigan, Hupfeld, Abagis and Shah built a questionnaire to measure it in adults and found that people with more ADHD symptoms reported more episodes. You can recognize it without any diagnosis, in a video game or a project you love.

## The good part and the cost

In hyperfocus you can clear in one afternoon what you had put off for weeks.

You pay for it later: the meal you skipped, the full glass of water, the unanswered message, the meeting you missed. And once you're in, switching to something else gets much harder.

## Why you don't remember to stop

To stop on time, you first have to notice that time has passed. In hyperfocus you put all your attention on the task, and you have none left over to think "I should stop."

Decide your exit before you start, and let something outside your head tell you when.

## Five ways to stop on time

### Decide when you'll stop before you start

Write it down with whatever comes next: "At 6 p.m. I stop and go for a walk." That plan is easier to keep than "just a bit longer."

### Use a signal you can hear

If knowing the time means looking at a clock, you won't look. You do hear a sound every 30 minutes, even with your eyes glued to the screen, and each ding makes you ask whether to keep going. To pick the gap, see how an [interval timer on Android](/blog/interval-timer-android/) works.

### Leave a note of where you were

Fear of losing the thread is part of what keeps you from stopping. Before you stop, write one line about the next thing you were going to do. Tomorrow you pick up from there instead of starting cold.

### Tie each signal to something physical

Drink some water or get up and stretch every time it rings. You skip both in hyperfocus, and neither takes more than a minute.

### Choose when to let it run

A Saturday with no plans can be the best time to disappear into a project. Decide ahead of time when that's fine, so you don't find out at 1 a.m.
