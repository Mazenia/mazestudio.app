An interval timer rings at a fixed gap, again and again, until you turn it off. You might want a reminder every 15 minutes to check you're still on the task you meant to do, or every 30 minutes to stand up from the desk. Android can get you part of the way with what comes on the phone.

## What the Clock app can do

The timer in the Clock app counts down once and stops. To hear it again you have to start it by hand, and that's easy to forget once you're absorbed in something.

The other option is a row of alarms at 9:00, 9:30, 10:00 and so on. It works, but a full working day at 30 minutes means about 18 alarms to create, and each one takes over the screen until you dismiss it. For a light reminder during the day that gets tiring quickly.

## What to look for in an interval timer app

- **It repeats on its own.** You set the gap once and it keeps going all day.
- **It follows a schedule.** If it rings from 9 to 6 and stays quiet at night, you never have to remember to switch it off.
- **It rings on the minute.** Battery optimization delays many apps. If your dings drift, read [why Android alarms don't go off on time](/blog/android-alarm-not-going-off-on-time/).
- **The sound is short.** A full alarm every 15 minutes wears you down. A short ding that doesn't ask for a tap is easier to keep on.
- **It respects vibrate.** When the phone is on vibrate, a buzz is better than a sound in a meeting.

## Which interval to choose

Every 15 minutes suits days when you lose track of time easily or work on something you tend to drift away from. It's the gap behind [the 15-minute rule](/blog/15-minute-rule/).

Every 30 minutes works well for desk work, posture breaks and long afternoons.

Every 60 minutes gives you a marker for each hour with fewer interruptions, like the [hourly chime](/blog/hourly-chime-app/) of an old clock.

If you're unsure, start with 30 and adjust after a day. When you stop noticing the ding, the gap is too short. When you look up and an hour has gone without you noticing, try a shorter one.
