There are endless morning routines online. Afternoons get much less attention, even though that is where many days come apart. Lunch runs long, a "quick look" at the phone turns into forty minutes, and it is 5 p.m.

Many people feel a dip in energy in the early afternoon. Instead of fighting it, you can plan around it.

## 1:00 to 1:30: a short reset after lunch

Don't jump straight into your hardest task. Take ten minutes to look at what is left for the day and pick the one thing that has to get done. Write it down where you can see it.

## 1:30 to 3:00: easy, active work

The slump is a good time for tasks that keep your hands busy: replying to messages, tidying files, errands, calls. Save deep thinking for later if you can. A soft chime every 30 minutes helps here, because this is exactly when time stretches without you noticing.

## 3:00: move

Stand up and go outside if you can. Five minutes is enough. Many people find their focus comes back after the slump, and a short walk helps you meet it.

## 3:15 to 4:45: the one thing

Now do the task you chose after lunch. Put the phone face down, close the tabs you don't need, and work in blocks. If you like the pomodoro technique, this is a good slot for two or three rounds.

## 4:45 to 5:00: close the day

Write what is still open and the first task for tomorrow. Close the laptop. This small ending stops work from leaking into the evening and makes tomorrow easier to start.

## Adjust the clock, keep the shape

Your hours might be different, especially with school runs or shifts. Move the times to fit your day and keep the order: a short reset, easy work, a walk, one focused block and a clear end.
