Studying has two opposite problems. Some days you can't settle and check your phone every five minutes. Other days you focus well, look up, and it's 1 a.m. with half the syllabus still untouched, because you spent three hours on the first topic. The ideas below help you get into the work and keep a sense of the time once you're in it.

## Decide when you'll stop before you start

Write the end time at the top of the page: "Until 7:30." A session with an end feels shorter, which makes it easier to start, and it gives you something to plan backwards from. If you have three topics and two hours, you already know each one gets about forty minutes.

## Make the first step small and concrete

"Study biology" is hard to start. "Read pages 40 to 52 and write five questions" is easy, because you know exactly what to do first and when you're done. Before you sit down, write the first step on a sticky note.

## Put the phone out of reach

Every look at the phone costs more than the seconds it takes, because getting back into a page of notes takes a while. Leave it in another room or in a bag. If you keep it near to know the time, a clock on the desk or a sound that tells you the time does the same job without the notifications.

## Work in blocks with a planned break

Blocks of 25, 45 or 50 minutes with a short break in between work for most people. The method matters less than the break having an end, because a ten-minute break on the phone easily becomes forty. Set the break's end before you start it. If you like the pomodoro technique, here's [how to do it without watching the clock](/blog/pomodoro-technique-bell/). And if you want to use the break to review, see how [spaced repetition](/blog/spaced-repetition/) works.

## Let a bell check in

A short sound every 15 or 30 minutes is a cue to ask yourself whether you're still on the topic you meant to study. If you've drifted into something else, you catch it within fifteen minutes, while most of the evening is still ahead. If you're on track, you carry on. This is the idea behind [the 15-minute rule](/blog/15-minute-rule/).

## Finish by writing where to start next time

In the last two minutes, write the next step for tomorrow, as small as the first one today. Starting the next session becomes much easier, and you can stop without the feeling that you're leaving things half done.
